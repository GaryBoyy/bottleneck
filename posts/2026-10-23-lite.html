Why I bought LITE

The laser maker at the center of the optics bottleneck, bought a third off its high.

LITELumentumOptics
What it does
Lasers and optical parts for AI data centers
Move
Bought Oct 22
Watching
Co-packaged optics orders and its November report

Lumentum makes the lasers that turn data into light inside high-speed optical modules. It's one of very few companies that can make these lasers at volume, and demand has been running ahead of supply all year.

Two newer products are what make it more than a parts supplier. The first is lasers for co-packaged optics, where the optical connection is built straight into the chip package instead of a separate plug-in module. The second is optical circuit switches, which route light between servers without converting it back to electricity. Management says order visibility now reaches four to five years out, and that its largest customer has stepped up co-packaged optics work for 2027 to 2029. Nvidia also owns a stake in the company.

It also fits the map of what I already own. AXT makes the indium phosphide wafers the lasers are built on, and in July Lumentum reserved AXT's capacity through 2031. AAOI builds finished modules. With Lumentum, I now own the wafer, the laser and the module.

That's also the main risk to the account: optics is now more than a quarter of it, and when optics crashed in 2001 it crashed harder than almost anything. For Lumentum itself, the risks are valuation and execution. Even after this drop, the stock is up nearly five times from a year ago, so any stumble in ramping new capacity will be punished.

It's about 6% of the account. Its next report, due in November, is the first test.
