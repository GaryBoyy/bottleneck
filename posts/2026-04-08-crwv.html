Why I own CRWV

An AI cloud built on contracts, and a balance sheet built on debt.

What it does

Added Mar 30
Watching
New contracts vs. new debt

CoreWeave buys AI chips in bulk, builds them into data centers, and rents the computing to companies training or running large models. It's a direct bet on one imbalance: demand for ready-to-use AI computing keeps running ahead of supply, and building your own takes years.

The contracts make this a business. Most capacity is sold on multi-year commitments signed with large AI labs before or while the hardware goes in. When CoreWeave borrows to buy chips, it borrows against signed contracts.

The balance sheet is the whole debate. The chips are bought with debt, and they lose value as new generations arrive. The bear case says the chips age faster than the loans get paid down, and if computing stops being scarce, prices collapse onto a pile of debt. The bull case says older chips keep earning on smaller workloads for years. Both make sense.

The other risk is concentration: a few customers make up most of the revenue.

I hold two lots, one from the winter and one added on last week's dip. It stays mid-sized because the debt does my sizing for me. My scorecard: are new contracts growing faster than new debt?
