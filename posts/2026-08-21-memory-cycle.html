How long can the memory boom last?

Prices are still rising, but more slowly. What would tell me the cycle is turning.

Memory makers are posting numbers nobody has seen before. SK Hynix's operating profit last quarter was more than six times what it was a year earlier. Sandisk's yearly revenue nearly tripled. Micron reports next month. My three memory positions are about 12% of the account, so the question is how long this lasts.

The case that it keeps going. AI demand for high-bandwidth memory is booked far ahead, HBM eats about three times the factory space of normal memory, and new factories take two to three years. DRAM contract prices are still rising, by about 13% to 18% this quarter according to TrendForce.

The case that it's peaking. That same forecast was around 60% last quarter, so growth is slowing fast. Phone and PC makers say they've hit their price limit. Korean makers are spending heavily on new capacity. And China is building its own supply, with domestic chipmaking tools now in production.

Memory cycles don't end when prices stop rising. They end when supply catches up while everyone is still confident. Here's what I'll watch:

  1. Contract prices flat for two straight months.
  2. All three DRAM makers raising spending plans at the same time.
  3. Customer inventories building up instead of running down.
  4. Chinese DRAM showing up in big-name phones and PCs.

None of these is flashing yet, though the first is getting close. I trimmed hard in June, so my memory weight is about half what it was then. I'll hold it there and let these signals decide the next move.
