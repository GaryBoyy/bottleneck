MarketsOctober 30, 2026

Big tech's spending holds, but cash is running thin

My sell signal didn't fire. A different warning light came on.

In September I set one clear sell signal: if two of the four biggest spenders cut their 2026 spending plans, I cut my AI hardware exposure by a third. This was the week they reported, so here's the check.

  • Alphabet kept its 2026 plan of $195 to $205 billion and said 2027 spending will rise significantly.
  • Meta still expects up to $145 billion this year.
  • Amazon still expects about $220 billion.
  • Microsoft spent less in the quarter than analysts expected, which it blamed on the timing of deliveries rather than a cut.

No one cut, so the signal didn't fire.

The new problem is cash

The spending is now bigger than the cash these companies bring in. Alphabet's free cash flow turned negative last quarter, about −$9 billion, and the stock had its worst day ever yesterday, down about 13%. Amazon's free cash flow has been negative over the past year. Meta plans to raise $95 billion by selling stock, and Microsoft is turning to the bond market. Today the chip index fell 7.2% after a weak outlook from Intel and a disappointing quarter from Amazon.

The richest companies in the world are now borrowing and selling shares to fund AI. That isn't a cut, but it makes the boom depend on markets staying willing to lend. So I'm adding a second signal: if any of the four lowers its 2027 plans, or a major AI debt deal fails, I start cutting.

Marvell and Lumentum

On Wednesday, Bloomberg reported that Marvell is in advanced talks to buy Lumentum in an all-stock deal, possibly announced as soon as next week. I own both. Marvell fell and Lumentum rose, since Marvell would pay with a lot of new shares. If it happens, my Lumentum shares turn into Marvell shares, and my two biggest groups, optics and chips, which are already about 40% of the account, would sit in fewer companies.

Also this week, the Fed held rates at 3.75% to 4%. Its next decision is December 9.
