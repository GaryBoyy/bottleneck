Why I bought AAOI

A small optics maker with its own lasers, riding the data center upgrade cycle.

AAOIApplied OptoelectronicsOptics
What it does
Optical transceivers and the lasers inside them
Move
Bought Mar 20
Watching
Orders from the big cloud customers

Applied Optoelectronics makes optical transceivers, the parts that turn electrical signals into light so data can move over fiber between servers and switches. Past a few meters, almost everything in a data center travels as light, and every link needs a transceiver at each end.

AI multiplies the number of links. In a training cluster, thousands of chips trade data constantly, so the network becomes part of the computer. That's why networking keeps taking a bigger share of data center budgets, and why speeds keep jumping from 400G to 800G to 1.6T. Each jump forces buyers to replace modules. More volume plus a replacement cycle is a strong setup.

AAOI is small, which is both the appeal and the risk. It makes its own lasers, the scarcest part of a transceiver. It has US factories at a time when buyers care where parts come from. A few big design wins would change its numbers a lot.

The risks are real. A couple of customers make up most of its sales, prices fall with every product generation, and the stock has gone nowhere for long stretches before. There is no moat here, only a window.

I bought a first lot this week and plan to add on weakness. If orders stall for two straight quarters, I'm out.
