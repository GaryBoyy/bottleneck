Back in: AAOI, Marvell and Bloom Energy

The first buys of the second half, made into a falling market.

Chip stocks have fallen for most of the past week. Since June 30 the semiconductor ETF is down about 11%, and so is my account. Today I spent about 40% of my cash.

  • AAOI. I bought back in at about 38% below the average price I sold at in June. Nothing in the business has changed. The stock got cut along with everything else that ran.
  • Marvell. Added at about 18% below my first lot from June 5, bringing it toward full size.
  • Bloom Energy, a new position: fuel cells that generate power on site at data centers. It's about 22% below its June high. Full post on Friday.

Why now: these were the names on my list, and their prices reached the levels I wrote down. Buying into a falling market feels bad, which is usually a sign the price is better.

The new risk is outside the chip world. The US and Iran traded strikes again today, the first serious fighting since the June agreement, and oil jumped. Higher oil means stickier inflation, which makes a Fed rate hike more likely, and expensive growth stocks are the first thing investors sell on that.

Cash is now about a quarter of the account. If prices keep falling I'll keep buying in steps, not all at once.
