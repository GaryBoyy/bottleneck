IREN and TeraWulf: same plan, different results

Two former bitcoin miners, one lesson about when revenue starts.

I own two former bitcoin miners turning their power sites into AI data centers. Since July they've gone in opposite directions. IREN is roughly back to my cost, while TeraWulf is about 25% below where I bought it.

IREN is further along. Microsoft accepted the first of four deployments under its five-year cloud contract, so revenue from that deal has started. In August IREN signed a second multi-year contract with a frontier AI lab, and this month its 2-gigawatt Texas site cleared an important step in the state's grid-connection process. Contracted annual revenue is now about $4 billion.

TeraWulf has the bigger single contract, the 20-year Anthropic lease, but money from it doesn't start until late 2027. Until then it has to raise and spend billions to build the campus, which means more borrowing and more shares. Insiders have also been selling. The market is charging for the wait.

I should have weighed this in July: for capital-heavy companies, when the revenue starts matters as much as how big the contract is. IREN's revenue is arriving now. TeraWulf's is a year away, with a lot of financing in between.

I'm holding both. TeraWulf is about 3% of the account, and the lease is real. But I won't add until the Kentucky build shows steady progress and most of the funding is in place.
