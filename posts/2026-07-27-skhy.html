HBM4 shipments for Nvidia's next chips

SK Hynix is the leader in high-bandwidth memory, the stacked DRAM that sits next to every Nvidia AI chip. Until this month, owning it meant buying shares in Korea. On July 10 it listed American depositary shares on the Nasdaq, so now I can own it directly.

Why add a third memory company when I already hold Micron and Sandisk? Because SK Hynix has the biggest share of the most important product. It supplied most of the HBM for Nvidia's current chips and is expected to supply the majority for the next generation, Vera Rubin. Micron and Samsung compete for second place.

The timing is uncomfortable. Memory stocks are in a sharp selloff, and the US shares are down about 15% since their first day. SK Hynix reports tomorrow, after a Korean broker warned it could miss on slower HBM4 shipments. I'm buying anyway, because I care more about HBM demand in 2027 than about one quarter's shipment timing.

The risks: this is a cyclical company near the top of a boom, Samsung is fighting hard to win back HBM share, and Korean stocks carry their own political and currency risk.

With this buy I've spent the last of my cash. That's not where I wanted to be, and it's the main thing I'm watching this week.
