Micron's $54 billion quarter

Record numbers, a year of visibility, and a hard look at my own trims.

MUMicron TechnologyMemory & storage
What it does
One of three DRAM makers; also makes flash
Move
Holding (trimmed Jan and Jun)
Watching
2027 HBM pricing and new capacity plans

Micron reported last night, and the numbers barely look real. Quarterly revenue was nearly five times what it was a year ago. Gross margin was about 87%, which is software territory for a company that runs factories. Next quarter's forecast is higher again.

Two details matter more than the headline. First, Micron says most of its high-bandwidth memory supply for 2027 is already under contract at significantly higher prices. That's a year of visibility in an industry that usually has none. Second, its data center storage business, on the flash side, has become a major business of its own. AI is pulling on both halves of the company.

Now the uncomfortable part. I trimmed Micron in January and twice in June under my size rule, and the stock is well above every price I sold at. My remaining shares are up about 360% from cost, but the position is much smaller than it would have been.

Was trimming wrong? The rule did its job. It moved money out of a position that had grown too big, before July cut memory stocks by a third or more. But I trimmed a cyclical company before the cycle showed any sign of turning. My consensus rule should have told me to wait, and the size rule overrode it. Next time a position outgrows its target while its cycle is still tightening, I'll trim less.

On current earnings, Micron is still the cheapest way I know to own AI memory. It stays, at about 6% of the account.
