Chips' worst day since April 2025, and a Micron buy that broke my cash rule

Democrats won Congress, chip stocks fell 12% in a day, and I spent cash I'd promised to keep.

Tuesday's midterms gave Democrats control of both the House and the Senate. On Wednesday markets took it well: the S&P 500 rose about 1.6%, Treasury yields fell, and oil dropped about 9% on bets that the White House would ease off the war with Iran.

Today went the other way. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell about 12%, its biggest one-day drop since April 2025, and the S&P 500 fell 4.4%. Weak outlooks from Qualcomm and Arm started it. Then a new fear spread: that a Democratic Congress could put new limits on data center construction. Micron fell about 17%. My account fell about 15%, its worst day this year, and is now about 15% below the total money I've put in.

The Micron buy

I bought more Micron today, about 37% below where it traded on October 1. At this price, the stock trades at less than five times the earnings Micron forecast for this quarter, annualized, and most of its 2027 high-bandwidth memory is already sold under contract. If the memory cycle holds even partly, the price looks too low.

But this buy broke two of my own rules. Cash is now about 6%, below the 10% floor I set in July after running out of cash at the bottom. And memory is now about a fifth of the account, nearly double the weight I said in August I'd hold. Micron alone is about 14%, my biggest position.

I'm writing that down plainly. The floor exists for days like today, and I spent it anyway because the price looked too good. If prices keep falling, I won't have cash to act, which is the same mistake I made in July.
