Where things stand

A rough week for AI stocks, a record for the market, and a snapshot of the account.

The S&P 500 set a record on Tuesday. My account fell about 6% this week anyway, because the week's big story hit exactly what I own.

The OpenAI number

On Thursday the Financial Times reported that OpenAI's annualized revenue was about $50 billion at the end of September, roughly $20 billion less than a figure that had circulated widely. Most of the gap is accounting, since OpenAI doesn't count sales made through cloud partners the way Anthropic does. But OpenAI is the biggest single buyer behind a lot of AI infrastructure, and the market sold first. Chip stocks fell about 4% on the week, and AAOI, AXT and Astera Labs each lost 9% to 14% in a day.

I didn't sell. The gap is about how revenue is counted, OpenAI is still growing very fast, and the same day Samsung guided to a record quarterly profit on memory demand. My sell signal hasn't changed since September: cuts to big-tech spending plans. None have come.

The rest of the world

September added only 29,000 jobs and unemployment rose to 4.2%, which lowered the odds of another Fed hike. The 10-year yield is still above 5.2%. Oil is near $90, and the war with Iran has no ceasefire in sight.

The account today

The account is worth about 36% more than the total money I've put in. On a time-weighted basis, which strips out when the cash went in, it's up about 84% this year against 14% for the S&P 500.

  • Optics: 24%
  • Chips and networking: 15%
  • Memory and storage: 13%
  • Compute and sites: 9%
  • Power and cooling: 7%
  • Defense, space and minerals: 5%
  • Cash: 27%

What's next

Third-quarter earnings start next week, and the big-tech spending updates come at the end of the month. The midterm elections are on November 3. I'll put cash to work only in steps, and never below the 10% floor.
