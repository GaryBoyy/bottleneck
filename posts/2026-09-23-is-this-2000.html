Is this 2000?

Comparing today's AI spending boom with the telecom bust, point by point.

Every week someone compares the AI boom to the telecom bubble of 2000. I own the suppliers, and suppliers got destroyed last time, so I owe myself a straight comparison.

What looks the same

  • Spending is outrunning revenue. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta plan to spend more than $700 billion on capital projects this year, up from about $410 billion in 2025. AI revenue is growing fast, but not that fast yet.
  • Some builders run on debt. Oracle borrowed heavily to build data centers for OpenAI, its free cash flow is deeply negative, and its stock had its worst week in 25 years in June. CoreWeave, IREN and TeraWulf all borrow heavily too.
  • Money goes in circles. Chipmakers invest in AI labs that then buy their chips, which flatters demand.
  • The key buyer is private. OpenAI has pushed its IPO to 2027, so the market can't see its books.

What looks different

  • The biggest spenders are rich. The four giants fund most of their spending from cash flow. In 2000, the fiber was laid by young, indebted carriers that went bankrupt.
  • The capacity is being used. By 2002 only a few percent of the fiber in the ground was lit. Today Nvidia says next year's growth is limited by supply.
  • Valuations are high but not extreme. Leaders like Nvidia trade at a fraction of the multiples Cisco reached in 2000.

What I take from it

The part that worries me: in 2001, optical component makers fell harder than almost anything. Optics is about a quarter of my account. If spending stalls, my stocks fall first and furthest.

So I'm setting one clear sell signal. If two of the four big spenders cut their capital spending plans, I cut the account's AI hardware exposure by a third, with no debate. Until then, the evidence says the demand is real.
