These two play the same role: the speculative end of the account. Each is a small company in an overlooked corner of the buildout, priced for doubt, and each could double or halve without surprising anyone. Being wrong on both costs little. Being right on either pays for the pair.

One Stop Systems builds rugged high-performance computers: AI-class hardware made to survive vehicles, aircraft, ships and field sites. The idea is that AI moves to where decisions happen, and autonomous and defense systems can't wait on a distant data center. Defense budgets drive demand, and won programs run for years. The risks are classic for a small defense supplier: lumpy orders and slow procurement.

POET Technologies is the more speculative one. It has spent years developing a way to assemble optical components on a wafer, using chip-style manufacturing instead of costly manual assembly. If it gets designed in at scale, the cost advantage is big. But revenue is early, the cash comes from selling stock, and photonics history is full of elegant technology that never reached volume.

Neither gets averaged down, so a speculative bet can never grow into a large one through stubbornness. Silence through year-end earns an exit.
