Why I bought WULF

A former bitcoin miner with a 20-year lease from Anthropic.

WULFTeraWulfCompute & sites
What it does

Bought Jul 13
Watching
The Kentucky campus staying on schedule

TeraWulf runs the same playbook as IREN: a bitcoin miner with power-ready land, turning itself into a landlord for AI computing. The difference is that TeraWulf already has the big contract.

Last week it signed a 20-year lease with Anthropic for about 400 megawatts at a new campus in Kentucky. The lease is worth roughly $19 billion over its first term, and more if Anthropic extends it. Delivery starts in the second half of 2027. At its New York campus, TeraWulf already hosts AI capacity for Fluidstack, with Google backing those leases.

I like the structure. A 20-year lease with one of the top AI labs looks more like infrastructure than a tech bet. Once the building is delivered, TeraWulf collects rent whether AI models get cheaper or not. That's the kind of revenue a former miner gets re-rated on.

The risks are about getting there. Building 400 megawatts takes enormous amounts of money, so I expect heavy borrowing and new shares. Construction delays would push revenue out. And one tenant is a lot of concentration.

I bought yesterday, a week after the announcement, once the excitement had faded. The milestone is simple: the Kentucky campus on schedule.
