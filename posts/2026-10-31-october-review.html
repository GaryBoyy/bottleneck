October review

My second-worst month of the year, and three buys that made me more exposed to debt.

October was rough. The account fell about 20% for the month on a time-weighted basis, while the S&P 500 fell 1% and the semiconductor ETF fell 14%. For 2026 it's still up about 51%, against 11% for the S&P 500 and 45% for the semiconductor ETF. On the total money I've put in, the account is up about 12%.

What hit it

  • A hot inflation report (3.7%) that kept another Fed hike on the table.
  • The CoreWeave bond scare, which spread to every company building AI data centers with borrowed money.
  • Two big chip selloffs: Lam's supply warning on October 22, and Intel and Amazon on October 30.
  • Oil: Brent rose about 14% in the month as tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz picked up.

None of my holdings rose in October. The biggest drops were IREN and One Stop (−41% each), CoreWeave (−37%), Sandisk (−34%), and AAOI and POET (−30% each). Vertiv held up best, down 4%.

My three buys

So far: Lumentum is up about 12% from my price, the TeraWulf add is up about 4%, and the CoreWeave add is down about 17%.

What I got wrong

For the second time this year, I bought into a falling market before it finished falling. The bigger mistake was what I bought. Compute and sites, my most debt-dependent group, went from about 9% of the account to about 19% in two weeks, right as debt became the market's main worry. The 10% cash floor held, so I still have about 15% in cash.

New limits

  • Compute and sites stays capped at about a fifth of the account.
  • No more adds to CoreWeave or TeraWulf until their next debt deals close on reasonable terms.

The account today

  • Optics: 28%
  • Compute and sites: 19%
  • Chips and networking: 13%
  • Memory and storage: 13%
  • Power and cooling: 7%
  • Defense, space and minerals: 5%
  • Cash: 15%

Coming up

The midterm elections are on Tuesday, and a Marvell and Lumentum deal could be announced the same week. Then come the jobs report, Lumentum's earnings, Nvidia's report in late November, the November 27 end of China's antimony export-ban pause, and the Fed on December 9.
