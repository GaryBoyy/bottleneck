Nvidia's record quarter, under a cloud

The numbers were huge. The stock fell anyway, because the worries are about trust and funding.

Nvidia reported last night, its first quarter under interim CEO Colette Kress. Revenue rose 103% from a year ago and data center sales 110%, and next quarter's forecast came in above analyst estimates. Kress raised the outlook for next year to about 75% growth. The new Vera Rubin systems brought in close to $20 billion in their first three months on sale.

The same day, the Wall Street Journal reported that the SEC has opened a civil investigation into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors. Neither the SEC nor Nvidia has confirmed it, and shareholder lawsuits have started. The stock fell about 3% today and is down more than a third from its October high.

For my suppliers, the report is good news. Vera Rubin uses the newest high-bandwidth memory, which helps SK Hynix and Micron, and every cluster built on it needs optical networking, which helps Lumentum and AAOI. Nothing in the numbers says AI demand is slowing.

But the market has stopped pricing demand alone. Between the CEO's exit, the SEC report, big tech's shrinking free cash flow and CoreWeave's debt scare, investors are pricing trust and funding risk. My stocks look cheap on earnings and keep falling, because earnings aren't what people are worried about.

My account is about 11% below the money I've put in. I'm holding without adding, with cash at about 5%.
