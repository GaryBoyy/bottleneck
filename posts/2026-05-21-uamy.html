Why I own UAMY

A tiny miner that is most of America's antimony supply.

UAMYUnited States AntimonyDefense, space & minerals
What it does
The only integrated US antimony producer
Move
Holding; sold half in February
Watching
Antimony prices and China's export rules

This is the strangest position in the account: the only meaningful American producer of antimony, a metal most investors couldn't place on a periodic table.

Antimony hardens lead in ammunition, goes into flame retardants, strengthens alloys and batteries, and clarifies the glass in solar panels. None of those uses has an easy substitute, so when supply breaks, price does the adjusting.

Supply broke in late 2024, when China, which dominates mining and processing, banned antimony exports to the US. Prices went vertical. China suspended the ban last November as part of the trade truce, but every shipment still needs a license from Beijing, and the suspension runs out this November. Defense planners now know a key input for ammunition runs through a rival.

UAMY owns the scarce thing: US mining and, just as important, US processing. In a market this small, a company this small is the whole American supply story. That cuts both ways. Expanding production is slow and expensive, and revenue depends on a commodity price that can fall as fast as it rose.

Its job in an AI-heavy account is to move on different news: geopolitics and munitions instead of data centers. I sold half in February. The rest is up about 40% from my cost.
