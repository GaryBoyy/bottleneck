MarketsNovember 28, 2026

The first crack in memory

Forecasters put a date on the end of the memory shortage, China moved the antimony deadline, and inflation kept rising.

It was a short holiday week with big swings. My account fell about 10% on Tuesday, lost a bit more on Wednesday, and got most of it back in Friday's half-day session. For the week it's down about 4%.

Memory's first crack

Memory has been my best layer this year, and Micron is my largest position. This week, for the first time, credible forecasters put a date on the end of the shortage.

  • Tuesday: a BNP Paribas analyst downgraded Micron. He now expects memory supply to catch up with demand by mid-2027, a year earlier than before, partly because Nvidia is reportedly putting less high-bandwidth memory into a future platform. Micron fell 11%.
  • Wednesday: TrendForce cut its 2027 memory forecasts because Chinese cloud companies reduced their orders. It still expects DRAM revenue to grow about 69% next year, but price increases to shrink each quarter and possibly stop in the second half. It now expects flash prices to fall.

Micron lost about 15% over those two days, then rose almost 8% on Friday.

I checked both against the sell triggers I wrote down. Micron's is contract prices going flat for two months, or all three DRAM makers raising capacity plans at once. Neither has happened. A forecast of flat prices in late 2027 isn't flat prices now. Sandisk's trigger is flash prices turning down, and that's now the forecast for 2027. It's a forecast, not a price yet, but it's close enough that both theses are now under review.

Memory is about a fifth of the account, nearly double the weight I said in August I'd hold. Micron reports on December 17. If it guides to slower price increases, I'll cut memory back toward 10%.

The antimony deadline moved

China's pause on its ban on antimony, gallium and germanium exports to the US was due to end Friday. On Tuesday Beijing extended it to January 27. UAMY fell 9%.

In August I wrote that if the pause was extended and antimony prices hadn't found a floor by year-end, I'd sell and use the loss against this year's gains. The first half has now happened. European prices have been flat since early September, about two-thirds below their 2025 peak, so they may have found that floor. UAMY is about 1% of the account. I'll decide before December 31.

The global picture

Inflation is still moving the wrong way. The Fed's preferred gauge rose to 3.8% in October, up from 3.4% in July, and third-quarter growth was revised up to 4.3%. Markets now see a December 9 rate hike as almost certain, and another in January as more likely than not. The US also proposed new tariffs on 14 economies, including Taiwan, but left memory chips out.

On Thursday the Financial Times reported that Nvidia told partners to stop shipping AI servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia while it checks that buyers there follow US export rules. Chinese companies had been renting Nvidia chips through data centers in those countries. Nvidia rose less than 1% on Friday while chip stocks rose about 4%. Huang testifies before the Senate Banking Committee on Wednesday.

Where things stand

The account is about 15% below the total money I've put in. On a time-weighted basis it's down about 24% in November and up about 14% for 2026, against 7% for the S&P 500 and 22% for the semiconductor ETF. Cash is about 6%.

I made no trades this week. The next two weeks will decide a lot: Huang's testimony Wednesday, the jobs report Friday, the Fed and Broadcom on December 9, and Micron on December 17.
