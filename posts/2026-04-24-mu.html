Why I own MU

A famously cyclical company with a structural reason this cycle could run longer.

MUMicron TechnologyMemory & storage
What it does
One of three DRAM makers; also makes flash
Move
Holding since fall 2025
Watching
HBM pricing and new supply

Micron is one of the three DRAM makers left, and a top flash maker too. For most of its history that made it a violent cyclical: you rented the stock for a year and a half and gave it back before the crash. My position from last fall is a bet that this cycle is different for structural reasons.

The first is high-bandwidth memory. Micron fought its way into the top AI platforms, and demand is so strong that supply is committed far ahead. HBM sells less like a commodity and more like a design win: qualified early, priced on value, and tied to the customer's roadmap.

The second is the factory math from my memory post. HBM uses about three times the capacity per bit, so the shift to HBM drains supply from the regular market while makers spend on converting capacity instead of adding it. Tight supply in normal memory plus premium prices in HBM is what makes memory makers very profitable.

What can break it is the cycle itself. If AI chip demand pauses, HBM orders get hit first, and this new discipline has never faced a real demand shock. Chinese producers are also adding capacity in older products.

My lot from last fall has roughly doubled, even after I sold some in January. It stays core for now, but it's first in line for the trimming rules I owe this journal.
