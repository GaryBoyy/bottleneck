What's behind the chip selloff

Five reasons chip stocks have fallen, and which ones I think matter.

The semiconductor ETF is down about 12% from its June 22 peak. Memory has been hit harder: Micron is down about 21% and Sandisk about 32%. My account is down about 16% since June 30, after a rough week and a partial bounce. Here's what I think is going on.

  1. Profit-taking. Memory and optics just had one of their best half-years ever, and a lot of investors own the same names. When everyone is in the same trade, the exit gets crowded.
  2. Memory prices are biting. Memory makers raised prices so fast that phone and PC makers are pushing back, and reports say Apple is lining up Chinese suppliers. High prices can shrink demand outside AI.
  3. Korea. A Korean brokerage cut its estimate for SK Hynix's quarter because of slower HBM4 shipments, and the stock had its worst day ever on July 13. Every memory stock followed it down.
  4. A lawsuit. A new class action accuses the three DRAM makers of limiting supply to raise prices.
  5. Oil and rates. Since fighting with Iran resumed on July 7, oil is up more than 25%. The 10-year Treasury yield is rising, and traders see a real chance of a Fed rate hike within the next two meetings.

The fifth matters most for valuations. Higher rates make future profits worth less today, and that hits expensive growth stocks first. The second matters for the cycle, and I'm watching it. The third and fourth look like noise to me, since one analyst's estimate and a lawsuit don't change HBM demand.

The real test comes next week, when Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta report and update their spending plans. If AI spending holds, this is a correction inside an uptrend. If it gets cut, my whole account has a problem, and I'll say so here.
