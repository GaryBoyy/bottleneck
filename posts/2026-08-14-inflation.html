Inflation is stuck, and the Fed is restless

July's numbers, a stalling job market, and why a rate hike is now on the table.

Two reports this month frame the problem the Fed is facing.

Inflation is stuck near 3.4%. Consumer prices in July rose 3.4% from a year earlier, a bit below June and well below April's 4.2% peak. Underneath, it's a split picture. Core inflation, which leaves out food and energy, fell to 2.4%, the lowest since early 2021. Gasoline is up about 27% from a year ago. Most of the inflation problem is the war, coming through oil.

Hiring has stalled. The economy lost about 23,000 jobs in July, and earlier months were revised down. Unemployment is still low at 4.1%, but the trend is weak.

A weak job market usually means rate cuts. This time, three Fed officials already voted to raise rates in July, and the committee is treating inflation as the bigger risk. Markets now price a real chance of a hike in September, which would be the first since 2023.

Why this matters for my account: almost everything I own is valued on profits years in the future, and higher rates make those profits worth less today. That doesn't change how much memory Micron sells. It changes what investors will pay for it, and the July selloff was partly that.

I've added new money recently, so cash is back to about a quarter of the account. It earns interest in a money market fund while I wait.
