Power is becoming the limit

Why electricity, not chips, may be the next bottleneck.

Every research thread I pull lately ends at the same wall: electricity. Generating it, moving it, and getting permission to connect to it.

Start with scale. New AI campuses are planned in gigawatts, a unit that used to belong to cities. One large campus can need the output of a full power plant. Meanwhile, a new grid connection in the US can take years. You can build a chip in months, but you can't rush a substation approval.

The builders' behavior proves the point. They're signing long-term deals with nuclear plants, paying to restart closed ones, generating power on site to skip the grid queue, and choosing locations by available power first. This spring's energy shock from the Iran war only raised the value of power that's secured and local.

Inside the building, the same pressure shows up as heat. Racks are too dense for air cooling, so the industry is moving to liquid cooling piped straight to the chips. That adds new equipment to every site. Transformers and switchgear now have long lead times too.

So I want the account's power exposure to be deliberate instead of accidental. The shortlist: an equipment maker that sells the electrical and cooling gear inside data centers, more generation exposure, and the power-site company I already own. The next buys follow the electricity.
