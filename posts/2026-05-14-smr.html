Why I own SMR

The first approved small reactor design, and a long wait for revenue.

Small modular nuclear reactors

Holding since January
Watching
A firm order from a named US customer

NuScale wants to shrink nuclear plants into factory-built modules that can be produced in series instead of built as one-off megaprojects. The pitch is that nuclear's problem was never the physics. It was construction.

Its most valuable asset is regulatory. It holds the first small modular reactor design approved by the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission, a process that takes years. Its scars explain the low price: its flagship US project was cancelled in 2023 after costs pushed the projected power price too high.

What changed is demand. AI campuses need steady, clean, around-the-clock power. Wind and solar can't do it alone, and gas clashes with the buyers' carbon goals. Nuclear checks every box, and big tech has shown it will sign long contracts and pay a premium for firm clean power.

Every risk here is the same risk: time. Real reactor revenue is years away, the company burns cash while it waits, and first-of-a-kind projects are where nuclear plans usually die.

So it's one of my smallest positions. I need a firm order from a named North American customer within a reasonable window. Without one, patience becomes an excuse.
