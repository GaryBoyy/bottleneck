The Fed hikes into 5% yields

The first rate increase since 2023, and what a 5% Treasury yield means for stocks like mine.

The Fed raised its benchmark rate by a quarter point yesterday, to a range of 3.75% to 4%. It's the first increase since July 2023 and the first under Chair Kevin Warsh. The vote was unanimous, and most officials expect one more hike before the end of the year.

Warsh's message was short: inflation has been too high for too long, and this summer's readings don't show real improvement. August inflation held at 3.4%. Energy is the main reason, but monthly core inflation also came in a little hot.

The bond market moved first. On Monday the 10-year Treasury yield crossed 5%, a level it had touched only briefly in 2023 and hadn't otherwise reached since 2007. Inflation worries, heavy government borrowing and the oil shock all pushed it there.

Why 5% matters for my stocks

A 5% yield is what investors can earn with almost no risk, and every stock has to compete with it. For a company valued on profits five or ten years out, a higher discount rate takes a bigger bite out of today's price. That arithmetic is why my account fell harder than the market on every rate scare this summer.

The flip side is that my cash earns more, and the bar for new buys is higher. A stock has to offer much more than 5% a year to be worth the risk. I'm fine with that bar. It keeps me from buying things just because they've fallen.
