Third-quarter review

Down 13% in a quarter the market rose. Still up about 88% for the year.

The third quarter was the hardest stretch since I started this journal. The account fell about 13% on a time-weighted basis, while the S&P 500 rose 2% and the semiconductor ETF fell 7%. For the year it's still up about 88%, against 12% for the S&P 500 and 69% for the semiconductor ETF.

The shape of it

July: −23%. August: +7%. September: +6%. From the early-June high to the July 29 low, the account fell about 40%. Since that low it has recovered about 38%.

What worked

All figures below are as of September 30.

  • Most of the July buys. AXT was up about 60% from my average cost, SK Hynix about 28%, and Marvell's July lot about 17%. As a group the July purchases were up about 13%, despite the timing.
  • Selling AAOI in June. The stock ended the quarter about 46% below my average June sale price, and I bought part of it back far lower.

What didn't

  • The June speculative buys. Planet Labs, One Stop and POET were down between 35% and 50% from cost.
  • TeraWulf. Down about 30% from my July price.
  • Pace. I spent all my cash in three weeks and had none left for the bottom.

Lessons

  1. Small, unprofitable companies fall hardest in a rate scare. My speculative tier should stay smaller than it was in June.
  2. Buying into a falling market needs a schedule as well as a price list.
  3. A good business can still be a bad buy at the wrong price.

Going into the fourth quarter, cash is about 26% of the account across sixteen positions. The biggest group is optics, at about a quarter of the account.
