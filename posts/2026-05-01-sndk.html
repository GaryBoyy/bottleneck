Why I own SNDK

A neglected spin-off meets the first AI-driven flash cycle.

Flash prices and data center demand

Sandisk returned to the public market in early 2025, spun off from Western Digital. Spin-offs are an old pattern for a mechanical reason: the shares land with holders who never chose them, index funds sell what doesn't fit, and few analysts cover the stock. When I bought last fall, that neglect was half the thesis.

The other half is the flash cycle. NAND has been the most self-destructive corner of the chip industry, but the version that came out of the last downturn is consolidated and has held its discipline. Sandisk has real scale through its long-running manufacturing joint venture with Kioxia, which splits the huge factory costs.

Then AI showed up as a new storage buyer. Training data, model checkpoints and AI output all land in data centers that increasingly want flash instead of spinning disks. A disciplined market plus a new structural buyer is the DRAM story one industry over, with less attention.

To be clear about what this is: a cyclical. When NAND turns, it crashes, and no AI story changes the math of oversupply.

It's the best trade in the account, up about five times from my cost even after sales in January and February. The plan, written down now: sell in stages into strength. Cyclicals reward the people who leave early.
